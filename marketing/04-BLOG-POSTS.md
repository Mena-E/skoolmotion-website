# 10 Questions Every Parent Should Ask a School Transportation Service

Choosing someone to drive your child is one of the most important decisions you'll make as a parent. Whether you're considering a bus service, carpool, or a dedicated school ride service, these are the questions that matter.

## 1. Are your drivers certified for school transportation?

In Massachusetts, drivers who transport school-age children must hold a 7D license — a state certification specifically for school transport. Ask any service whether their drivers hold this license. If they hesitate, walk away.

At SkoolMotion, 100% of our drivers are 7D certified. No exceptions.

## 2. What background checks do you run?

A basic background check isn't enough. Look for services that run:
- Criminal background checks (CORI in Massachusetts)
- Sex Offender Registry checks (SORI)
- Driving record reviews
- Reference checks

SkoolMotion runs all four before any driver completes their first ride.

## 3. How do you verify my child's identity at pickup?

This is the question most parents forget to ask — and it might be the most important one. If a driver pulls up to school, how do they confirm they have the right child?

SkoolMotion uses a 4-digit verification PIN. Your child confirms the code before boarding. No PIN, no ride.

## 4. Can I track my child's ride in real time?

You shouldn't have to wonder where your child is. Ask whether the service offers live GPS tracking that you can view from your phone — not just a vague ETA, but actual real-time location.

SkoolMotion provides live GPS tracking on every ride with pickup and drop-off notifications.

## 5. What's your on-time rate?

Reliability matters more than almost anything else. A service that's late 10% of the time means your child misses the bell once every two weeks.

SkoolMotion's on-time rate is 99.8%.

## 6. Are vehicles inspected regularly?

Massachusetts requires commercial vehicles to be inspected annually. But for a service carrying children, that's a bare minimum. Ask how often vehicles are checked.

SkoolMotion vehicles are state-inspected twice per year — double the requirement.

## 7. Do drivers have medical clearance?

A driver's physical health matters for safety. Ask whether drivers are required to pass a medical exam.

Every SkoolMotion driver has a physician-signed medical clearance on file.

## 8. What happens if something goes wrong?

Ask about the service's emergency protocols, insurance coverage, and whether they have a 24/7 support line.

SkoolMotion provides 24/7 live monitoring and support for every ride.

## 9. How flexible is the scheduling?

Life changes. Can you book a ride the same day? Skip a day without penalty? Change pickup times?

SkoolMotion offers daily subscriptions, on-demand rides (book up to 7 days ahead), and easy modifications with no cancellation hassles.

## 10. What do other parents say?

Check reviews, ask for references, and look for a proven track record with real families.

# The True Cost of the School Carpool: Time, Gas, and Sanity

Let's do the math on what school transportation actually costs you — even when it's "free."

## The Time Cost

The average school pickup-and-drop-off routine takes 45-60 minutes per day. That includes:
- Driving to school (15-20 min in Boston traffic)
- Waiting in the pickup line (10-20 min)
- Driving home or to the next activity (15-20 min)

Over a 180-day school year, that's **135 to 180 hours** — the equivalent of 3.5 to 4.5 full work weeks spent in your car.

If you coordinate carpool with other families, add the time spent texting, rescheduling, and managing the inevitable "sorry, can't do Thursday this week" messages.

## The Gas Cost

At current gas prices in Massachusetts (~$3.50/gallon) and an average round trip of 8-12 miles:
- Daily gas cost: $3-5
- Monthly: $60-100
- Annual: $540-900

And that's just one child. Multiple kids at different schools? Double it.

## The Stress Cost

This one doesn't have a dollar figure, but every parent knows it:
- Rearranging work meetings around the 3:15 bell
- The guilt of being late (again)
- Coordinating with 3 other families who all have different schedules
- The anxiety when the bus doesn't show and you're 20 minutes away

A 2024 survey found that 67% of parents with school-age children reported that school transportation logistics cause "significant daily stress."

## The Opportunity Cost

Those 135-180 hours per year? That's time you could spend:
- Working (at $30/hr, that's $4,050-5,400 of earning potential)
- Exercising, resting, or just breathing
- Spending quality time with your kids instead of stressful car time
